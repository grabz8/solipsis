\section{Related Works}

The Web3D consortium have originally the project to create a freely navigable online world \cite{pesce}. Unfortunately, due to technical contraints, such as bandwith limitation, the VRML standard was only use to encode simple 3D contents in order to visualize them on web sites. Only, related works and bandwith increase make it possible to draft the architecture of a Web3D, metavers or cyberspace that have to be decentralized to reach a sufficient scalability level.
\subsection{Compression and adaptive 3D Streaming}
Many research works have been focused on compression of 3D models, and on adaptive streaming methods that allow to progressively transmit the required 3D contents visualized from the current viewpoint. \\
First, existing 3D compression algorithms use both techniques adapted from the 1D and 2D cases (like wavelets, entropy coding, and predictive coding), and completely different approaches that take advantage of the properties of 3D surfaces (like Edgebreaker, Subdivision Surfaces, and triangle strips)\cite{}\cite{}. Otherwise, parametrizable solutions, that provide intuitive modeling tools, are widely used to create avatars, complex creatures, or vehicles in games. More complex 3D contents can be create thanks to procedural modeling solutions that have the drawback to require a reconstruction process executed on the fly on the client side. Parametrizable and procedural modeling provide very good compression rate compared to usual mesh compression algorithms, but no generic solutions exist to model a great variety of objects.\\ 
The second solution consists in filtering the 3D contents required to visualize  the scene from a given viewpoint. Indeed, a complete download of a huge 3D scene is not necessary to render with optimal details the virtual environment from a given viewpoint. First, the area of a 3D model projected on the screen depends on its size, but especially on its distance from the viewpoint. In this way, many solution have been proposed to adapt the resolution of a 3D models to the current viewpoint \cite{Funkhouser}. Continuous levels of detail  allows to transmit refinement progressively as the viewpoint approaches 3D objects \cite{Hoppe}\cite{Luebke}. Secondly, into huge and complex 3D scenes, most of 3D objects are occluted by other ones for a given viewpoint. Thanks to a offline computation of 3D objects visible from regions resulting from a partitionning of the navigation area, server can signifanctly reduce the amount of data that have to be sent to the client, without affecting the visual quality \cite{}\cite{}. Unfortunately, the visibility filtering have to be prohibit during flying over navigation, since occultations are clearly fewer. Finally, these filtering methods provide multiresolution functionnalities allowing an adaptive 3D streaming of the virtual environments.\\
\subsection{Centralized architectures}
Several architectures take advantage of levels of detail presented previously \cite{npsnet}\cite{massive}\cite{ring}\cite{dive}. More recent commercial plateforms such as Google Earth and Second Life have known a tremendous succes, and have brought to light a real public interest for online virtual environments. The first one allows a navigation into and over a model of the earth, with terrain and buildings. Google earth takes advantage of an adaptive streaming of terrain textures \cite{tanner}, associated with a multi-resolution wavelet based representation for terrain, and static levels of detail for buildings. The second one provides an advanced level of a social network service combined with general aspects of a metaverse. The Second Life client integrates modeling tools allowing the creation of the virtual environments by the users. More than fifty virtual worlds appeared these last years, and all are based on a centralized architecture.\\
Generally, huge environments are partitionned according to a grid in regions, that are managed by dedicated servers, called region servers. Unfortunately, in order to synchronized the game states of the world for all connected clients, most of process such as collision detection, physics computation, animation, are executed on the server side. Moreover, the clients have only one source that can provide them the 3D models of the scene required for visualization. Thus, the number of clients which can navigate in a region managed by one server, is clearly limited.
